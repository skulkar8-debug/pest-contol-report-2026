Executive Summary
A Recurring-Service Industry With Active Consolidation, Where Independent Operators Still Define the Sourcing Opportunity
Part I: Market Context and Investment Thesis
The U.S. pest control industry combines a large recurring-service revenue base with a highly localized operating structure. External research estimates $29.7 billion of industry revenue in 2026 after 3.4% annual growth from 2021 to 2026, with slower but positive growth forecast through 2031. Commercial and institutional work adds a compliance-driven revenue layer that is less discretionary than residential demand.
STOC Advisory’s location-level analysis base contains 19,993 observed location records. The underlying source data provides an ownership type for 19,901 records: 16,916 independent targets and 2,985 records grouped as PE-backed platforms, public chains, family-owned consolidators, or franchise / licensed networks. Independents represent 85.0% of that ownership base, so market-level prioritization is more useful than broad list-building.

A $29.7B recurring-service market with moderate growth, resilient demand, and a meaningful commercial compliance layer
Pest control is a mature service industry with moderate growth, resilient recurring demand, and a meaningful commercial compliance component. External research sizes revenue at $29.7 billion in 2026, supported by 34,076 enterprises, 36,840 establishments, and approximately 178,000 employees. Common household pests account for 40.8% of revenue; termites, rodents, and outdoor pests each contribute roughly 14%. Market Structure and the Consolidation Opportunity
Why the Sector Attracts Consolidators
Fragmentation, recurring compliance-driven demand, and provider density that define the consolidation opportunity
Pest control rewards operating density and local reputation, but the provider base remains highly fragmented. In that setting, acquisitions can provide access to existing territories, customer relationships, technicians, and service capabilities rather than building new branches from the ground up. Commercial compliance supports recurring work, and denser provider supply, larger purchasing volumes, shared technology, and centralized administration can support cost evaluation across travel, labor, purchasing, and administration.

Add-On Markets
Plain Market Rules Separate Established Add-On Markets From Large Low-Network Independent Markets
Transparent CBSA groups built from independent count, network share, and parent / network count, not one combined score
The data supports plain market groups rather than a single blended score. Established add-on markets have at least 100 independent locations, at least 10% network share, and at least 10 parent companies or networks. Large independent markets with low network presence have at least 100 independent locations and network share below 10%. A market can support add-on sourcing even when several platforms or chains are already present, and a market with little network presence can still be highly competitive if it contains many independent providers.
